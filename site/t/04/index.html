THE WORK BEHIND THE VISION

Not just a city.

Zistshahr is a research-led proposal connecting urban form, social relations, open science, independent small-scale production, ecology and the systems that sustain everyday life. This visual journey illustrates concepts; it is not a claim that a physical city has already been built.

02 / THE OPEN-SCIENCE PRINCIPLE — PROJECT CATALOGUE

Knowledge should
multiply agency.

In the project’s normative proposal, open science means both designing technologies that smaller, independent producers can actually use, and sharing the resulting know-how. Free information alone cannot overcome a technology's capital-intensive design.

01Begin with lived needsResearch questions arise from households and everyday production.
02Build usable toolsDesign for accessible scales of production, not only large industry.
03Open the knowledgeMake discoveries available for adaptation and replication.
04Share the benefitsEnable community-funded R&D to serve beyond its funders.
PROVENANCE The open-science principles are adapted from the project-provided Persian catalogue “دانش آزاد”. These are proposed goals, not evidence of achieved outcomes. The accompanying presentation “Beyond-Market Science” discusses value creation, innovation and public investment, including Mariana Mazzucato’s work.

THE FIRST QUESTION IS NEVER HOW TO BUILD.

Before the form,
the human vision.

Zistshahr asks a foundational question: which social relations should a city make possible? The comparison below is a proposed interpretive framework developed in the project's research materials—not a universal or exhaustive classification of societies.

01 / HOBBES · 1651

“Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words.”

Leviathan, XVII. A reference point for a theory of order mediated by sovereign power, not a complete description of Hobbes.
02 / ADAM SMITH · 1776

“Led by an invisible hand to promote an end...”

The Wealth of Nations, IV.ii. A point of departure for thinking about decentralized exchange and unintended social outcomes.
03 / IMMANUEL KANT · 1784

“The unsocial sociality of man.”

Idea for a Universal History, Proposition IV. The tension between sociability and antagonism is a problem for social design.
